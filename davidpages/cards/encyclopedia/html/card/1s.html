The Figurehead

The most iconic playing card. It's the only one that deviates from the design of others of its rank.

Why is it like that?

In 1711, England started taxing decks of playing cards (again). To show that the tax had been paid for each deck, the A♠ was hand-stamped (presumably, just because it was the top card of a fresh deck). I guess it wasn't that robust at stopping forgery, so in 1765, the system changed. Card makers could no longer make their own A♠s; they had to print 51-card decks, then purchase intricately decorated "duty aces" from the Stamp Office. Forging aces was punishable by death; in 1805, Richard Harding was hanged for ace forgery. Perhaps this is why the A♠ is known as the ::skull:: Death Card...

In 1862, the duty ace was officially retired, but of course by then people had grown fond of the fancy card, and manufactureres continue to ornament A♠s, usually with some manufacturer information.
